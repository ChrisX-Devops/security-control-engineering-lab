# Honest Positioning Document

## My Positioning Statement

> "Cloud security and compliance automation
>  with IT/OT boundary awareness."

This statement is the only one I use externally.
It defines exactly what I do and signals what I understand
without overclaiming what I have not done.

---

## The Claim Test

Before making any claim about my capabilities, I apply this test:

1. Can I defend it with proof?
2. Have I done it in a real environment?
3. Would a mistake here cause physical consequences?

If I cannot answer (1) and (2) with yes, I do not claim it.
If (3) is yes and I lack proven OT experience, I do not claim it.

## What I CANNOT Claim Yet

### OT Security Operations
- I do not secure industrial control systems
- I do not assess live SCADA environments
- I do not configure PLCs or work with field devices
- I do not perform OT penetration testing

**Why:** I have no hands-on OT experience. Mistakes in OT can
cause physical consequences including injury or death.
Honesty here protects clients and my reputation.

### IEC 62443 Formal Assessments
- I do not perform IEC 62443 compliance assessments
- I do not certify systems against IEC 62443

**Why:** This requires OT engineering experience and often
formal certification I do not yet hold.

### Industrial Protocol Analysis
- I do not analyze Modbus, DNP3, or EtherNet/IP traffic
- I do not perform protocol-level OT security testing

**Why:** This requires specialized tools and OT lab experience.

## When Asked About OT Capabilities

### If asked: "Can you secure our OT environment?"

**Honest answer:**
"My focus is your IT environment and the boundary between
your IT and operational networks. I understand IT/OT
boundary risk and can identify where your network
segmentation needs attention.

For security work inside the operational environment
itself, that requires OT-specific expertise. I am
building that knowledge through ISA CCST certification,
but for current engagements I would partner with an
OT specialist for anything inside the OT layer.

What I can deliver is everything on the IT side and
honest assessment at the boundary. Want me to walk
you through what that looks like?"

### If asked: "What is your OT experience?"

**Honest answer:**
"My hands-on experience is in IT security. I am pursuing
ISA CCST to formalize my understanding of control systems
concepts. I can speak the vocabulary and understand the
architecture, but I would not represent myself as an
OT security engineer until I have real OT environment
experience to point to.

What I bring is conceptual fluency that helps me design
better IT systems and communicate with OT teams. The
hands-on OT work is on my development path, not my
current capability."

## The Cost of Overclaiming

Overclaiming has predictable consequences:
- Lost credibility when caught
- Failed engagements I should not have taken
- Potential safety incidents in OT contexts
- Reputational damage that compounds

The cost of honest positioning:
- Some prospects walk away
- Slower initial revenue growth
- Patience required while building real experience

The benefit of honest positioning:
- Trust that compounds with every interaction
- Sustainable career growth
- Differentiation from people who overclaim
- Sleep at night

## Weekly Self-Audit

Every Friday I ask:

1. Did I make any claim this week I cannot defend with proof?
2. Did I let any prospect believe something untrue about my capabilities?
3. Did I take any work that would require capabilities I do not have?
4. Did I update my honest positioning when my capabilities expanded?

If yes to any: correct it immediately and document what I learned.
